## 2. Five shared patterns
1. 2:15–2:50 long.
2. The product (or its finished result) on screen within ~0–30 s.
3. A single concrete first sentence.
4. Person and agent editing one shared, live surface; several stage a human approval.
5. None shows a measured result against a baseline.

## 3. The person and the scale (verified, evidence.md)
- UNFPA India Ageing Report 2023: 10.5% of Indians were 60+ in 2022; "By 2050, the share of older persons will double to 20.8 percent".
- LASI Wave 1 (2017-18), Table 5.3: 5.7% of people 60+ live alone (women 8.5%, men 2.5%). Reasons: "having no children and having children who reside away due to education, work, or marriage."
- HelpAge India, June 2025, 10-city urban survey: "66 per cent of the elderly find digital tools as 'too confusing'"; 40% own a smartphone.
- No real elder could be filmed or interviewed before the deadline, so the elder and her grandson are **illustrative personas**, labelled on screen; the app and both devices are real.

## 4. What this film borrows (one line each)
- From the winners: the product working within the first ~25 s (the two-device moment), one concrete first sentence.
- From the gap in the winners: a measured result against a named baseline (browser-control only vs Chaupal actions; shaped vs unshaped text).
- From the base repo's film: nothing visual; its arc (statistic → activities in turn) is exactly what we avoid. We lead with the family at the same board, Hindi, and Agent-Native's one action set.
- From Crossbeam: real footage of the world first, a bed under the voice that lifts for the finale montage.
- From Meld: the guard staged as an event (the stale move refused, the send held for her yes).
- From Comacard: one deliberate silent beat before the biggest reveal (before the move lands on the phone).
- From TokenIQ: an honest label on every number (case and run counts read from the eval manifest, "OCR exact match"), the music entrance marking the product.
