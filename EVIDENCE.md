# Evidence: who Chaupal is for, and what we can and cannot claim

Evidence review date: 9 November 2026. Every source below was re-opened on that date. Quotes are exact.

## Source order

1. Indian national statistics and ministry policy.
2. Peer-reviewed or public research on national data (LASI).
3. State or local programme material.
4. Journalism, only for lived context the national tables don't capture.

Each row separates four things: what the source says, what Chaupal does about it, and what the source does not let us say.

## Evidence map

| Some elders live alone; far more live without their children. | LASI Wave 1 (2017–18), Table 5.3: **5.7%** of people 60+ live alone (men **2.5%**, women **8.5%**); **20.3%** live with a spouse and/or others but no children. The family link is part of the product, not an add-on. | Household type does not measure loneliness, need or risk. Applying 5.7% to 2022's 149 million gives roughly 8.5 million people; **that is our own estimate**, mixing a 2017–18 rate with a 2022 population. |
| Children moving away is a main reason elders live without them. | LASI: "The reasons for living without children are mainly having no children and having children who reside away due to education, work, or marriage." A LASI-based study found "**36 percent** of older parents have at least one migrant child, and **35 percent** are empty nesters" ([Sarkar et al., SSM – Population Health, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10523011/)). | A grandson in another city can open a link and sit at the same board. Nothing leaves her house without her yes. | The study's "migrant child" definition mixes "not in household" with "outside the district, state or country", and its own text is inconsistent. We found no verified figure for children living specifically in another city or abroad. |
| Interfaces, not ability, are the barrier. | HelpAge India (June 2025, 5,798 people in 10 cities, 30% aged 60+): **66%** of elders find digital tools "too confusing", **51%** avoid them for fear of errors, **40%** own a smartphone, **13%** use the internet. ([Outlook Money, 14 Jun 2025](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/digital-divide-among-elderly-66-find-digital-tools-too-confusing)) | She speaks in her own language and the activity appears. Menus, modes and coordinates are never her first task. | Urban sample, reported second-hand. Says nothing about whether voice or agents fix it. |
| Games are already social infrastructure for elders. | Kerala's Pakal Veedu day-care centres keep "chess and carom boards" ([India Ageing Report 2023](https://india.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/20230926_india_ageing_report_2023_web_version_.pdf), §3.8.2). Delhi's 2026 Vayo Anand centres will offer "carrom, ludo, chess, cards" ([Outlook Money / PTI, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/delhi-to-start-senior-citizen-recreation-centres-from-may-yoga-indoor-games-health-check-ups-for-seniors)). In Hyderabad's A.S. Rao Nagar, "senior citizens are opting for carrom, chess" ([Deccan Chronicle, 2016](https://deccanchronicle.com/current-affairs/190116/senior-citizens-opt-for-carroms-and-chess.html)). | Shatranj with Hindi piece names: the board elders already know, played at home with family or the agent. | Programme and press material. Does not show elders want to play on a screen. |
| The village chaupal is a real place, and not everyone's. | In Harsana Kalan, Haryana, the men "are often at the chaupal (village square), playing cards. The women are never seen there." ([PARI, 21 Apr 2020](https://ruralindiaonline.org/article/for-that-grandson-we-had-four-children)) | The name is a promise: a square she can sit in too, at home. | One village. The men are not described as elderly. |
| Creative activity is part of elder programmes. | Elder groups run "festival celebrations ... recreational activities" ([India Ageing Report 2023](https://india.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/20230926_india_ageing_report_2023_web_version_.pdf)); a Bhubaneswar activity centre ran "storytelling and writing"; Dignity Foundation's Chai Masti centres run "hands-on activities" in 30 centres ([Dignity Foundation](https://dignityfoundation.com/chai-masti-centres)). | Calligraphy and rangoli are offered as practice she continues herself, not as content she watches. | **We found no credible source naming calligraphy or rangoli in an Indian senior programme.** Their place in the product rests on their cultural role, not on programme data. |

## What Chaupal must not claim

- That it reduces loneliness, improves health or memory, or builds digital skills.
- That elders asked for it, or that it was tested with elders (it was not tested with an elder before submission).
- That any family, centre or organisation uses it.
- That a household living alone means a lonely or vulnerable person.
- Any number not in this file or in [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md).
